<p>
    Two thoughts on this Saturday:
</p>
<p>
    Firstly, I feel as though my brain is melting. Short-form content is truly a fascinating medium, but I really need to... stop watching it.
    I should be reading instead; anything really, besides mindlessly scrolling. I mean, everyone is scrolling these days, so in a way that's
    just participating in the grand culture of the time, but uh... It makes me feel bad. So I will stop (I say: a liar). I need to read way more.
    I have too many unread books on my self. What's even the point of <span title="TO BE READ BY ME!!!">them</span>! Writing this here is perhaps
    a resolution, although I will reserve such strong words because I lack hope.
</p>
<p>
    Secondly, AI is making me sad. It's interesting to me; the development of AI seemed poised to replaced artists, and while it does seem to
    be cutting into the job market of some art-field positions, I think that that whole push has largely struggled to find its legs. People are still
    offput and offended by AI art being pushed on them by corporations. Yes, opinions will change as we are continuously drowned in the slop, but honestly
    I expect the technology to always be seen as "cheap" in that regard.
</p>
<p>
    Really, what I'm feeling all melancholy about is code. AI tools for software developers are much further along. And no wonder. I remember most of the worthwhile
    papers I read about the tech as it developed during my undergrad were pushing the tech to understand code more than anything else. A technology developed by developers,
    it's no wonder it has learned their language best. Of course, it really isn't perfect, but in terms of adoption, it seems to be much further along than AI "art".
    It is becoming increasingly difficult for me to argue against its use as the people around me use it. I have dabbled occasionally, and I can't lie and say that AI
    technologies <em>haven't</em> helped me out of a bind occasionally. In terms of enhancing search, they can be helpful. I mean, I didn't know that Webworkers were even
    a thing until Copilot showed me them.
</p>
<p>
    But...
</p>
<p>
    Two things. Sometimes when I try and search an answer, I find myself mired in a back-and-forth with Copilot or Gemini or whatever. And I find myself
    unintentionally allowing myself to run in circles, getting answers I <strong>KNOW</strong> are wrong. And I must step back and reassess the whole line of questioning,
    in order to reorient what I actually am looking for and finally arrive at an answer.
</p>
<p>
    And it feels like the artistry of programming is being sapped by these tools. Culturally, people seem more willing to accept code being written by AI. Art generated by AI
    is soulless. It is wrong and uncanny and honestly just a slap in the face to the viewer. But code? Code is a practical thing. Software either works or it doesn't, right? 
    So whether it was vibecoded or whatever, who <span title="on a practical level we should care. I mean, good god, security, anyone?">cares</span>? But... I don't know.
    What draws me to programming <em>feels</em> spiritual, in some sense. I find the creation of software systems to be deeply satisfying. I find beautiful code to be beautiful, and i
    am gratified when I write it. It feels as though some kind of joy will be lost as these AI coding tools become the norm. I had to disable VSCode's Github Copilot integration to write
    these entries for goodness sake! It's nice when it helps me add a comma to the end of every entry of a list, but it tries to generate my own experiences for me in the text here! 
</p>
<p>What a nightmare. What a nightmare indeed...</p>
